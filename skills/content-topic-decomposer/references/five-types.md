# The five content types

Read this when generating ideas in step 3. For each type: the job it does, questions that surface ideas, a quality bar, and the usual failure.

## Tutorial: clarity
**Job:** the reader leaves able to do or understand something they could not before, in one sitting.

**Shapes:** step-by-step walkthrough, concept explainer, framework, decision guide ("when to use A vs B"), pitfall list, annotated example, FAQ on one narrow confusion.

**Questions that surface ideas**
- What do beginners get stuck on in the first hour?
- What is the shortest path from zero to a first visible result?
- What would I draw on a whiteboard for a smart friend from another field?
- Which term do people use confidently but misunderstand?
- What did I have to unlearn?

**Quality bar:** names its reader and their starting point; one outcome per piece; includes a concrete example, not only definitions.
**Failure:** the encyclopedia dump. Cover less, deeper. Split a big tutorial into a series, one node each.

## Case: credibility
**Job:** prove the method is not just a concept, because someone did it and something changed.

**Shape:** starting state, what was done, what changed (with numbers or before/after), what did not work, what was learned. Include the failures; a case with only wins reads as marketing.

**Questions**
- What have I (or a client, student, team) actually done with this?
- Which result surprised me, in either direction?
- What did the first attempt look like, and the last?
- Is there a public example I can dissect with evidence?

**Quality bar:** verifiable specifics (what, how many, how long, before vs after) and an honest limitation.
**Failure:** vague results ("it got much better"), or invented ones. If the creator has no case yet, design one: a small experiment, a reproduction of a public result, or a teardown of someone else's public work, clearly labelled as such.

## Opinion: recognizability
**Job:** show what you believe and do not believe, so the right readers recognize you and the wrong ones leave.

**Shape:** "Common belief → my stance → why → where I could be wrong." One claim per piece.

**Questions**
- What mainstream advice do I disagree with, and what happened that made me disagree?
- What is overrated, and what is underrated?
- What do I do differently from most people in this field, and why?
- What would I tell myself a year ago?

**Quality bar:** arguable (a thoughtful person could disagree), grounded in experience or evidence, bounded (states when it does not apply).
**Failure:** contrarian for its own sake; or "it depends" with no position. Both are forgettable.

## Story: warmth and memorability
**Job:** make the reader feel something and remember the person behind the topic.

**Shape:** want → obstacle → choice → change. Zoom into one moment (a failed run at 2 a.m., the day a client pushed back) rather than the whole journey. Include what it felt like.

**Questions**
- When did I first get this badly wrong?
- What was the moment something clicked or broke?
- Who said something that changed how I work?
- What did this cost me (time, money, confidence)?

**Quality bar:** one specific scene, a real feeling, and an ending that changes something; the lesson, if any, is implied or short.
**Failure:** a chronology of events with a moral stapled on; or a humblebrag. Small and specific beats big and vague.

## Resource: savability and shareability
**Job:** give the reader something to keep and pass on.

**Shapes:** checklist, tool list with opinions, template, reading path, glossary, "my setup", behind-the-scenes workflow, decision flowchart, cheat sheet.

**Questions**
- What do I open repeatedly that others would love to have?
- What did I wish existed when I started?
- Which scattered tips could be one page?
- What does my real workflow look like, step by step?

**Quality bar:** usable without reading anything else; curated (why this one, for whom, what to skip); dated or versioned so it can be updated.
**Failure:** a dump of links with no judgment. Curation is the value.

## How the types change with the topic kind

| Kind | Tutorial | Case | Opinion | Story | Resource |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Knowledge / technical** (ML, programming, finance) | concept + minimal working example | an experiment, benchmark or project with results | stance on a method, tool or common practice | a debugging night, a wrong assumption | config templates, checklists, reading lists, datasets |
| **Craft / skill** (cooking, photography, fitness) | technique demo | before/after of your or a student's work | what the standard advice gets wrong | the failure that taught the technique | gear list, routine template, shot list |
| **Commercial / service** (consulting, shops, products) | how to choose or use | client or customer results, with permission | positioning on pricing, process, industry norms | founder decisions, turning points | calculators, templates, behind-the-scenes process |
| **Lifestyle / experience** (travel, parenting, home) | how-to for a situation | a trip, month or project documented honestly | take on a trend or norm | the moment that changed a habit | itineraries, packing lists, budgets |
| **Creative** (writing, art, music) | process breakdown | a work analyzed or a project retrospective | taste and craft beliefs | the struggle behind a piece | prompts, references, templates |

When the creator lacks clients or results, "case" can be a self-experiment or a public teardown. Say so in the plan instead of leaving the cell empty.
