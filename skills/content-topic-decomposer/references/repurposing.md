# Repurposing one experience into several pieces (一鱼多吃)

One real experience contains a tutorial, a case, a story and often an opinion and a resource. The trick is that these are not copies in different lengths. Each version has a different protagonist, opening and ending. If three posts differ only in word count, readers feel they read the same thing three times.

## 1. Extract the raw material first
Before writing any version, make a fact sheet of what actually happened. Ask the creator for it if it is missing; never fill gaps yourself.

- **Goal**: what were you trying to do, and why?
- **Starting state**: tools, data, constraints, what you believed at the time.
- **Steps taken**: in order, including dead ends.
- **Numbers**: before/after, durations, sizes, costs.
- **Decision points**: where you chose A over B, and why.
- **Mistakes and surprises**: what went wrong, what you didn't expect.
- **Feelings and moments**: the scene you remember most.
- **Artifacts**: templates, configs, checklists, screenshots you made along the way.

## 2. Apply a lens per version

| Version | Protagonist | Opening | Middle | Ending | Cut |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tutorial** | the reader ("you") | the outcome the reader wants | repeatable steps, with the why of each | a checklist or next step | personal timeline, side stories, feelings |
| **Case** | the creator, a client or a project | the starting state and the goal | what was done, in order, with numbers | the result, limits, what you'd change | step-by-step instructions for others to follow |
| **Story** | the creator as a person | one specific scene | want → obstacle → choice | a change in the person; the lesson only if brief | most method detail and numbers |
| **Opinion** | the creator's stance | the common belief | the experience as evidence for the stance | when the stance doesn't hold | everything not supporting the claim |
| **Resource** | none; the object itself | what it is and who it's for | the template/checklist/config | how to adapt and where it can break | narrative |

## 3. Fan out across formats
Pick one anchor format for the richest version (usually the long article for the tutorial or case), then cut downward:

long article → graphic post (key steps or numbers) → short video (one hook + one point) → livestream (live Q&A on the same material)

Each cut needs its own hook. Don't paste a paragraph into a caption.

## 4. Link the versions
Tell readers where to go next. The story ends by pointing to the tutorial; the tutorial links the case as proof; the case links the resource. This turns scattered posts into a series and moves readers through the stages in `reader-stages.md`.

## Example chain (shape only)
Raw material (a made-up illustration from an unrelated field): "I spent a week finding out why my sourdough kept collapsing; the cause was proofing in an over-warm kitchen."
- Tutorial: "How to check your dough temperature before you proof" (reader as protagonist, checklist ending).
- Case: "One week, one loaf: what the warm kitchen did to my results" (creator as protagonist, with the real before/after photos and times).
- Story: "The morning I realized the recipe was fine and the kitchen was the problem" (one scene, one feeling).
- Opinion: "Check the environment before you change the recipe" (stance, with this case as evidence).
- Resource: "Proofing time-and-temperature chart" (the artifact itself).

The example shows structure only. Never carry its details (causes, outcomes, numbers) into a real plan; use the creator's actual facts, or leave a slot.

## Keep every chain complete
Give every hero experience at least the tutorial, case and story versions, each with its protagonist and hook, in the same compact table, so the second and third heroes are as usable as the first. Opinion and resource spin-offs are optional extras. If the creator has not yet supplied the facts a version needs, still list the version and say which facts are missing.

## Rules of thumb
- Two or three hero experiences are enough to fuel a month of content. Quality of material beats number of ideas.
- If the raw material is thin, say so and propose a way to gather more (run the experiment, interview a user, note a week of decisions) rather than padding.
- Each version should survive being read alone.
