# Formats

Choose the format after the type and stage, not before. Four formats are covered here. They are platform-agnostic on purpose: a "graphic post" might be a Xiaohongshu note, an Instagram carousel or a LinkedIn document, and platform specifics change too often to hard-code. If the user names a platform, adapt lengths and hooks to it using what you know, and say where you are unsure.

## Best fit

| | Graphic post | Short video | Long article | Livestream |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tutorial | ✅ steps, diagrams, checklists | ✅ one technique, demonstrated | ✅ deep, searchable | ⚠️ live walkthrough, rewatchable only if recorded |
| Case | ✅ before/after, numbers | ✅ result first, then how | ✅ full process with evidence | ✅ live review of a real project |
| Opinion | ✅ one bold claim + support | ✅ talking head, fast | ✅ nuanced argument | ✅ debate, Q&A |
| Story | ⚠️ works if image-led | ✅ spoken, personal | ✅ best for scene and feeling | ⚠️ fireside style |
| Resource | ✅ the most savable | ⚠️ tour, then link out | ✅ the reference page | ⚠️ walk through, pin the file |

✅ natural fit, ⚠️ possible with care.

## Notes per format

**Graphic post**
- Strength: skimmable and saveable; carries diagrams, comparisons and checklists well.
- Shape: a first frame that states the payoff, one idea per frame, a last frame that says what to do or read next.
- Avoid: text walls on images; more than about one idea per frame.

**Short video**
- Strength: shows a person; strong for strangers (stage A) and demos.
- Shape: the hook in the first seconds (result, claim or question), one point, a clear end. Show the thing, don't describe it.
- Avoid: slow intros; trying to teach a whole framework in one clip. Make a series instead.

**Long article**
- Strength: depth, search discoverability, nuance; the home for the anchor tutorial, full case, and reference resource.
- Shape: a title with a concrete object, a promise in the first paragraph, headings that can be skimmed, one worked example.
- Avoid: padding. If the idea needs 800 words, don't stretch to 3,000.

**Livestream**
- Strength: live questions reveal what readers actually struggle with; good for stage C and D, where people want to interrogate.
- Shape: a loose agenda, a live demo or teardown, time reserved for questions; record it and cut highlights.
- Avoid: using it as the first touch with strangers; it rewards an existing audience.

## Choosing quickly
1. Ask what the reader needs to *see*: a number, a diagram, a face, a process, a scene.
2. Ask where the reader is (stage A favours short and visual; stage D tolerates long).
3. Ask what the creator can produce consistently. A format they dread will not last.
4. Offer a primary format and one secondary for each idea; the secondary becomes the reuse path (see `repurposing.md`).
