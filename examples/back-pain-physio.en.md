# Content map: back pain for desk workers

First, the likely reason you're stuck. Exercise posts are all one content type: tutorials. Tutorials make people understand something, but they don't make strangers stop scrolling, prove that you get results, or make anyone remember you. The five types in this plan each do a different job (clarity, credibility, recognizability, warmth, savability). You've treated about 200 desk workers over 6 years, and that is the raw material for four of the five. You haven't used it yet.

## 0. Assumptions
- **Audience:** office and remote workers with recurring or on-and-off back pain, not severe or acute. They know the basics ("sit up straight", "do some stretches") and have probably tried a YouTube routine.
- **Goal:** grow an audience that trusts you. I'm assuming that may later mean clients, but I haven't built the plan around selling.
- **Real material:** about 200 patients over 6 years, plus what you've learned in clinic. I don't know any details, so every case and story below is a **slot**: it names the kind of experience to look for, and you supply the facts. I have not invented any outcomes, causes or quotes.
- **Platform:** Instagram carousels and short videos. Long article and livestream appear only as optional extras.
- **Topic kind:** a mix of craft/skill (physical technique) and service/health, so credibility and safety count for more than usual.
- **A note on patient material:** use cases only with consent or fully anonymised, and check your professional body's rules on testimonials and health claims. Keep a "see someone in person if…" line on anything that touches symptoms.

## 1. Topic nodes
1. **Why desk work and pain go together:** what's actually going on, and what's myth (posture, "sitting is the new smoking").
2. **First questions:** what you ask before you give any exercise.
3. **Red flags:** when it's not a "desk" problem and needs checking.
4. **Setup and equipment:** chair, sit-stand desk, monitor, laptop.
5. **Movement through the day:** breaks, variety, the 3pm slump.
6. **Flare-ups:** the first 48 hours, and what's normal.
7. **Exercises and recovery:** the part you already cover, in context.
8. **Mindset and trust:** fear of movement, getting a diagnosis, what a good first appointment looks like.

**Fan-out:** a single question can become a series: one carousel per sub-question, a video per myth, and a resource that links them.

## 5. Suggested order
1. **Open (strangers):** O1 or S1 as a short video, plus O2 as a carousel.
2. **Anchor (just learned):** T2, "3 questions before any exercise", so people see how you think.
3. **Prove:** C1 (Hero 1), then C2 (Hero 2).
4. **Collect:** R1, the flare-up decision card, linking T3, T4 and C1.
5. **Keep a rhythm:** roughly one exercise post for every three or four posts of other types, rather than the other way round. Once the other types are carrying growth, your exercises will reach more people because they'll have more reason to trust you.

If you only do three things this week: do the **C3 tally** (it gives you real numbers), film **O1**, and choose which patient to use for **Hero 1** (with consent).
